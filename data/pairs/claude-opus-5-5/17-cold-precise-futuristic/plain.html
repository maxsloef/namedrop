01 / The problem

Custom widgets break the tools built for commodity parts.

Your ERP knows a widget has a part number. It doesn't know the bore tolerance, which three suppliers can actually hold it, or that the shipment is sitting at a cross-dock in Memphis.

Failure mode A

Specs live in PDFs.

Drawings get revised, emailed, and re-revised. Suppliers quote against Rev B while engineering signed off on Rev C. Nobody notices until parts arrive.

Typical cost11% of orders reworked
Failure mode B

Orders live in inboxes.

Quotes, approvals, and lead-time promises are scattered across buyers' email. When someone's on vacation, so is your purchasing process.

Typical cost9.6 days per PO cycle
Failure mode C

Shipments live nowhere.

Supplier portals, carrier sites, and spreadsheet trackers each hold a fragment. You learn about delays when the line supervisor calls.

Typical cost2–4 line stops per quarter

03 / Configure

Catch the bad spec before it becomes a bad shipment.

Every parameter is checked in real time against what your approved suppliers can actually produce. If a tolerance narrows your supplier pool to three, you'll know before the RFQ goes out, not after.

.01

Parametric, not pictorial

Dimensions, materials, and finishes are structured data. Suppliers receive exact values, not a drawing to reinterpret.

.02

Revision control that holds

Once Rev C is released, Rev B can't be quoted, ordered, or received. The system enforces it, not a reminder email.

.03

Engineering and ops, same screen

Design owns the spec. Operations owns cost and lead time. Both see the trade-offs as they happen.

The way it usually goes

Most widget orders are held together by email threads and good memory.

i.

Specs drift between drawing and delivery

A tolerance changes in one spreadsheet but not another. The supplier builds what they were sent, and the plant receives something close, but not quite right.

ii.

Nobody can say where an order actually is

Status lives in inboxes and phone calls. Planners chase updates on Friday afternoons instead of planning next month.

iii.

Expediting becomes the operating model

When delays are discovered late, the only fix is freight you didn't budget for. Over a year, it quietly becomes one of your larger line items.

How Acme works

Three steps, done properly.

Acme doesn't ask you to change how your plants run. It gives each part of the process a proper place, so nothing important sits in someone's head.

Step one

Configure

Build each widget from your approved materials, finishes, and tolerances. Acme checks every configuration against supplier capabilities before it's ever sent, and keeps a versioned record of every change.

Step two

Order

Send purchase orders to one supplier or split them across several. Approvals route to the right people automatically, and quantities, prices, and lead times are confirmed in one place, not five.

Step three

Track

Follow every order from production through freight to the receiving dock. Acme notices slippage early, when there's still time to adjust calmly, and tells the people who need to know.

The platform

Built for the long, ordinary middle of manufacturing.

Not the launch or the crisis, but the thousands of routine orders in between. That's where Acme earns its keep.

Spec library

One approved source of truth for every widget your company buys, with version history and who changed what, and when.

Supplier network

Invite your existing suppliers at no cost to them. They confirm orders and post updates without changing their own systems.

Early drift alerts

Acme compares every order against its plan and flags changes days earlier than a phone call would, with enough time to act.

Multi-plant ordering

Consolidate demand across sites to earn better pricing, or keep plants independent. Either way, everyone sees the same picture.

ERP connections

Two-way sync with SAP, Oracle NetSuite, Epicor, and Microsoft Dynamics. Purchase orders and receipts stay in step without re-keying.

Quality & compliance

Attach certificates, inspection reports, and material traceability to each lot. SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 certified.

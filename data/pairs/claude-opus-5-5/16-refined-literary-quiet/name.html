Preface

Every manufacturer knows the particular weariness of a widget order gone astray: the bore diameter mistyped in an email, the revised drawing that never reached the supplier, the pallet that is somewhere between Toledo and the loading dock and nobody can say quite where. The part is small. The delay it causes is not.

Acme was built to remove that weariness. We give operations teams a single place to define a widget precisely, place the order with confidence, and follow it through every hand it passes. Nothing is decorative; each field, each status, each notification earns its place by preventing a mistake.

What follows is a short account of how it works. It should take about four minutes to read, and rather less to decide whether a conversation would be worth your while.

Chapter I

The widget, defined once

Begin with the part itself. Acme’s configurator lets your engineers set dimensions, materials, tolerances, finishes, and certifications in a structured form that suppliers read exactly as you meant it. No attachments to lose, no PDFs to re-key.

Rules you define, such as minimum wall thickness for cast aluminium or approved coatings for food-grade lines, are checked as the widget is drawn up, so impossible or non-compliant parts are caught before anyone quotes them.

  • i.Parametric templates for brackets, couplings, housings, and fasteners
  • ii.Engineering rules enforced at the moment of entry
  • iii.Full revision history, with sign-off from the right people

Chapter II

From quote to purchase order

Once a widget is defined, send it to one supplier or to five. Quotes return in a common format, side by side, so the comparison is of price, lead time, and capacity rather than of handwriting and file types.

Approve a quote and Acme writes the purchase order into your ERP, applies your approval thresholds, and confirms receipt with the supplier. Reorders of an approved part take a single line: the widget, the quantity, the date it is needed.

  • i.Comparable quotes from your approved supplier list
  • ii.Purchase orders written directly to your ERP
  • iii.Blanket orders and scheduled releases for recurring parts
Fig. 3. Three quotes, set in a common measure.
In the margin Suppliers update status from a simple page. No logins to manage, no software for them to buy.
Chapter III

Every widget, every hand

From the moment a purchase order is confirmed, each widget carries its own record: machined, inspected, packed, shipped, received. You see where the order stands without asking, and the supplier is spared the daily chasing call.

When a date slips, Acme tells the people who depend on it, with enough warning to act. Certificates of conformance and inspection reports arrive attached to the part they describe, and are there, in order, when the auditor asks.

  • i.Status for each line item, not merely each order
  • ii.Early notice of delays, routed to whoever is affected
  • iii.Quality documents filed with the part, permanently
Fig. 4. The progress of a single line item.
